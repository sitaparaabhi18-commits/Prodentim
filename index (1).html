Your mouth is an ecosystem

Hundreds of microbe types live in your mouth. Most are harmless or helpful. Problems tend to start when plaque builds up and the balance tips toward less friendly bacteria.

Sugar, smoking, dry mouth, some medications and skipped cleaning all shift that balance. Brushing, flossing and dental visits remain the proven foundation.

Why probiotics are discussed

  • The idea is to add beneficial strains to the mouth
  • Research on oral probiotics is real but still emerging
  • Results vary by strain and by person

Frequently asked questions

Is ProDentim FDA approved?

The FDA does not approve dietary supplements before sale, and supplements are not approved to treat disease.

Does it cure cavities or gum disease?

No, and we make no such claim. See a dentist for any dental condition.

Can I skip brushing if I take it?

No. It does not replace brushing, flossing or dental visits.

Are there clinical trials on ProDentim?

None are verified here. Studies on individual strains exist but are not proof for this product.

What evidence and safety limits should I know about?

Research on oral probiotics is emerging and mostly concerns individual strains, not this product. Results vary. Check with a healthcare professional if you are pregnant, nursing, immunocompromised, allergic to an ingredient or have a medical condition.

Are there side effects?

Probiotics can cause mild digestive effects in some people, and allergies are possible. Check the label and ask your doctor.

Where should I buy it?

How soon will I notice results?

There is no verified timeline. Be cautious of anyone promising one.
